# OpenAI firings of staff who worked on the investigation (Oct 2026)

Researched 2026-11-22 for s-8/s-9. Loaded: Fortune (9 Oct 2026, Emily Forlini) and SF Standard
(24 Oct 2026, Juan Pablo Pérez Burgos). Not loaded: CNN (HTTP 451), NPR 9 Oct and 23 Oct (timeouts),
the WSJ originals, the joint letter PDF and the X posts, so those details come via Fortune / SF Standard.

**Takeaway:** sourced that OpenAI fired three safety staff, two of whom worked on the Hugging Face
investigation (one as METR's technical point of contact). **The reason is disputed.** OpenAI cites
mishandled sensitive information and says the firings were not about safety concerns; the staff say
it was about their METR communication and prioritising safety. On a slide, state both sides; never
assert that OpenAI fired them *for* the investigation.

## Who
- **Tomek Korbak**: "was the technical point of contact for METR, the third-party research firm that
  OpenAI asked to conduct an independent audit of the incident" (Fortune). Worked on the HF investigation.
- **Mikita Balesni**: worked on the HF investigation (Fortune); role not stated.
- **Jasmine Wang**: "a program manager on OpenAI's safety team" (Fortune); HF link not stated.
- When: WSJ first reported on 1 Oct 2026; Fortune (9 Oct) says "last week". Exact date not given.

## OpenAI's stated reason (verbatim, via Fortune)
- 1 Oct: the "individuals mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures",
  "violating our policies and breaking the trust essential to our work."
- 8 Oct: "Our internal investigation uncovered a significant breach of trust beyond what's outlined
  in the letter they published"; "we stand by the decision to not continue their employment."
- Internal memo (Fortune: probably Mark Chen): "I want to be very clear that these decisions were not
  about raising safety concerns or speaking out."
- Fortune paraphrase: OpenAI "did not link the firings to its communications with METR or the Hugging
  Face incident". SF Standard paraphrase (from WSJ): unapproved Signal use "wasn't the only reason".
- OpenAI says it is "still committed to working with third-party safety assessors" (Fortune).

## The staff's account (verbatim, via Fortune / SF Standard)
- Korbak (X): told verbally he "was fired because of the way I communicated with METR"; "Talking to
  METR was my job."
- Balesni (X): they "were fired for prioritizing safety over the near-term interests of OpenAI as a
  corporation."
- Wang (X): OpenAI cited her access to an executive's email (granted for recruiting, removal not
  completed by IT; Fortune paraphrase); "We were not the first to be pushed out of OpenAI under
  suspicious circumstances."
- Signal (SF Standard, their account): security asked to review their Signal chats with a METR
  contact; disappearing messages meant nothing to review; "a week later, they were out."
- Leak context (SF Standard, from WSJ): OpenAI suspected a leak of The Information's Astra story;
  the staff deny leaking; Ryan Greenblatt (METR/Redwood investigator) denied being the source.

## Effect on independent investigation (verbatim)
- Joint letter (via Fortune): firings "are chilling the open culture OpenAI has prized"; "our firings
  may be used to justify ending OpenAI's work with METR."
- Wang (X): "Raise concerns or work closely with outside safety groups, and you could be next, without
  being told why."
- Korbak (SF Standard): "OpenAI was quite worried that METR was going to be unnecessarily adversarial";
  "METR was worried that OpenAI was going to maybe withhold some data from them."
- Neel Nanda (X, via Fortune): "is a sign of a highly unhealthy culture."
- METR did not comment (Fortune).
