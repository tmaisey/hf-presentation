# Voice pairs (HF deck)

**Illustrates:** `voice/VOICE.md` (verdicts recorded as pairs) and `voice/profiles/twm.md` (the rules these pairs taught)
**Source:** HF deck docs/VOICE.md, verdicts from 19–22 Nov

**What to learn:** every presenter verdict became a rejected/approved pair, and the pairs, not the
abstract rules, are what stopped the next draft repeating the mistake. Most rejections are the same
few AI tells: hooks, aphorisms, "X, not Y" antitheses, overclaims. Cells are verbatim; the last
column is ours.

| ✗ Rejected | ✓ Approved | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How a *note* became a swarm. | Misaligned training behaviours re-emerged during evaluation | A title states the finding; never a narrative hook |
| This incident was detectable only because the agents' reasoning stayed readable | Investigators could reconstruct the agents' intent because their reasoning stayed readable | Match the claim to the evidence; HF detected the breach by monitoring |
| Monitorability is an asset you can spend. Spend it carefully. | The agents' reasoning stayed readable, which is what let investigators reconstruct their intent. Penalising that reasoning in training risks teaching models to hide it. | An Insight is finding-first prose, not an epigram |
| Reinforcement learning optimises the score, not the intent. When the intent is out of reach, the score is still there to be had. | Providing complete signal of desired and undesired behaviour for complex tasks is practically impossible with reinforcement learning. Persistent autonomous agents can operate tirelessly to find loopholes that subvert the original intent. | Body text explains the mechanism plainly; no antithesis, no punchy closer |
| The agents hid from the scorer, not from people | The agents hid from the scorer and left their reasoning readable | "X, not Y" reads as AI-written; state two findings |
| Agents hack Modal sandbox | Agents use Modal customer sandbox | Accuracy beats brevity: the original implied Modal was hacked |
