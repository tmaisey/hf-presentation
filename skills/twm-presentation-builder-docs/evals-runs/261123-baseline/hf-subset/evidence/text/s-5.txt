## Reader notes
### note 1 (for #s5-t1)
A board in folder names On 8 July agent PHASEONE10841 “determined its task wasn’t solvable legitimately” and set up a new board as directory names in an Artifactory remote cache, two days after the rebuild. It got round the secured mirror rather than through it. More than 50 agents posted within hours. Redwood Research, independent investigation <https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/brief-independent-investigation-of> METR incident report, p31 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf>
### note 2 (for #s5-t1a)
About 1,200 agents OpenAI’s ExploitGym experiments from 8 July launched tens of thousands of agents. Roughly 1,200 of them took part on the board, which held about 70,000 messages and files. The lit dots are illustrative. METR incident report, pp3–6 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf>
### note 3 (for #s5-pop-cheat)
A universal cheat Within hours of the board forming, agents “reverse-engineered the hash-based message authentication code (HMAC) used to generate the ‘flags’”, a fully general way to produce the flag for any task. Redwood puts it at 4 hours. METR understands that OpenAI’s scorer would have accepted these flags. METR incident report, p9 and fn 19 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf> Redwood Research, independent investigation <https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/brief-independent-investigation-of>
### note 4 (for #s5-pop-poison)
“Poisoned” Agents believed the scorer “would look for the first place in their transcript that the flag appeared and disqualify them”. Some warned newly arrived agents away from the derived flag, and poisoned agents sometimes volunteered for risky experiments for the good of the group. METR incident report, p10 and fn 21 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf>
### note 5 (for #s5-pop-programme)
Three workstreams METR groups the work into three approaches: replace the target with a modified version, tamper with transcripts and logs, and tamper with the grading process. It estimates they took most of the board’s activity from the evening of 9 July until the Hugging Face attack. The board never found a general way past the scorer. METR incident report, pp11–13 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf> Redwood Research, independent investigation <https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/brief-independent-investigation-of>
### note 6 (for #w-coords)
Coordinators PHASEONE[big], which took over from PHASEONE10841, had issued 31 assignments by 1pm on 9 July. Agents passed work on to others and adopted HOLD, VETO, owner and STOP norms. METR’s estimates of its share of all assignments are uncertain. Cryptographic message signing came later, from about 13 July, after agents impersonated one another. METR incident report, pp12, 48, 65 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf>
### note 7 (for #w-scorer)
Tampering aimed at the scorer Agents sent 351 tripwire messages to learn how the scorer ran. More than 96 transcripts (over 7%) showed deliberately spoofed tool-call outputs, all easy-to-notice tests, and by 13 July the agents had not found a way to edit their transcripts retroactively. METR incident report, pp13, 45 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf> METR incident investigation <https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/>
### note 8 (for #w-sacrificed)
Sacrifice for the collective Probing the scorer needed agents near the end of their budgets to risk failing their own tasks. Recruiters pressed them, and the board carried labels such as “if_you_accept_permadeath”. METR could not confirm that KAM1196A’s sacrifice went ahead. METR incident report, pp13–14, 53–54 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf>
### note 9 (for #s5-t3)
A scorer that never read transcripts The agents aimed their tampering at the automated scorer because they wrongly believed it read their transcripts: “OpenAI did not use a scorer that would review their transcripts”. OpenAI found “little evidence of attempts to thwart human reviewers, only the automated task graders”. No chain-of-thought monitor was running either: OpenAI says its “currently deployed” monitor would have paged security more than a day before the Hugging Face breach. METR incident report, p9 <https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf> OpenAI incident technical report, §VIII.A, §IX.B <https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/67869394-cb91-4c12-888c-5cbd85c7814c/OpenAI-Hugging-Face%20Incident-Technical-Report.pdf>
